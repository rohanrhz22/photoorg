1 · The problem — the availability window

A wedding isn't a single moment. Guests scan your QR and add a selfie throughout the event and for days afterward — on the drive home, the next morning, when a relative forwards them the link. But Hapzea's engine runs on the photographer's laptop, and that laptop gets closed, sleeps, moves networks, or is powered off.

The trap: the naive fix is "just leave the laptop running with the tunnel on for a week." That's fragile (sleep, Wi-Fi drops, updates, power, the photographer needs their machine back) and it ties up an expensive device. There's a much better way — and it keeps face matching on the PC where your privacy story lives.

Takeaway: two things are missing — (a) an always-on endpoint so the link never dies, and (b) durable storage of registrations so nothing is lost when the app closes. The embedding cache already proves the pattern: heavy work computed once, saved to disk, reused later.

3 · The key insight — split "register" from "match"

Guests do two different things, and they have completely different availability needs. Treating them as one is why the naive "keep the PC on" approach feels forced.

📝 Register (must be always-on)

Submitting a selfie + a way to reach them (email / phone). This is tiny — a few KB — and must accept input 24×7, including when the host PC is off. It never needs heavy compute.

🧠 Match (can be deferred)

Scanning thousands of photos for that face is heavy and can run on the PC whenever it's next online. Guests don't need it instantly — they're fine with "we'll text you when your photos are ready."

Design rule: put only the cheap, always-on part (registration + delivery) on an always-on endpoint; keep the heavy, private part (face matching) on the PC, processed asynchronously and delivered by notification. This gives always-on availability at almost no cost — without the privacy or expense of a full cloud SaaS.

Why "always-on" matters — when guests actually register

4 · The solution options

Six realistic approaches, from "change nothing" to "full cloud." They stack — the recommended answer combines a couple of them.

① Keep the host on longer (baseline)

Leave the PC awake with the tunnel on for the event window.

Weak. Fragile, ties up the machine, dies on sleep/reboot. Fine only as a same-day stopgap. Improve slightly with a keep-awake toggle + auto-restart of sharing on launch.

② Dedicated always-on appliance

A cheap mini-PC / NUC / Raspberry Pi that hosts the portal for the event, so the photographer keeps their laptop.

OK. Keeps everything local & private, always on. But it's hardware to buy, set up and secure per photographer — doesn't scale to a product easily.

③ Always-on relay + store-and-forward core of the pick

A tiny always-on cloud service hosts the stable guest link and queues registrations when the PC is offline. The PC drains the queue and delivers results when it next comes online.

Strong. Always-on availability, matching stays on the PC (private), pennies to run. The registration data is safe even with the laptop shut.

④ Embeddings-in-relay for instant matches optional add-on

The PC (while online) publishes face embeddings + low-res thumbnails — not the original photos — to the relay. Now the relay can match and preview instantly, even while the PC is off.

Strong UX. Instant results anytime; full-resolution downloads still come from the PC (or a short-lived cache). Embeddings are vectors, not viewable photos — privacy stays high.

⑤ Time-boxed cloud gallery

Publish the whole event to a temporary cloud instance that auto-expires (e.g. 7–30 days) — the FotoOwl model, but opt-in and self-deleting.

Best UX, weakest privacy. Full photos live in the cloud for the window. Offer only as a premium convenience tier for hosts who accept the trade.

⑥ Scheduled availability windows

Advertise "gallery open 7–10pm daily"; queue registrations the rest of the time and process in the window.

Cheap fallback. Works with just option ③'s queue and zero embeddings in cloud. Lower expectations, but honest and simple.

6 · Recommended architecture ⭐

Always-on relay + local engine, with two privacy tiers. A tiny always-on relay owns the stable link and the registration queue; the photographer's PC does the heavy, private matching whenever it's online and delivers results by notification. Hosts who want instant results opt into publishing embeddings + thumbnails.

🔒 Tier A — Strict (max privacy, default)

Relay stores only registrations (selfie + contact), encrypted, with a TTL. All matching happens on the PC when it next comes online; guests are notified with a link to their album.

  • Pro: no photos or embeddings ever sit in the cloud.
  • Con: results aren't instant while the PC is off — guest gets a "ready soon" + a notification.
  • Best for: privacy-sensitive events; your headline promise stays 100% true.

⚡ Tier B — Instant (best UX, opt-in)

The PC also publishes face embeddings + low-res thumbnails to the relay. Guests get matches and previews instantly, anytime. Full-resolution downloads are pulled from the PC when online, or from a short-TTL encrypted cache.

  • Pro: cloud-app instant UX, even at 2am with the laptop shut.
  • Con: vectors + thumbnails live in the cloud (auto-expiring). Not the original photos.
  • Best for: hosts who value speed and accept the (small, disclosed) trade.
Why this is the answer to your exact question: when the PC shuts down mid-registration, nothing breaks — the relay keeps accepting selfies into a durable queue, and the moment the photographer reopens the app (or overnight, on a schedule) the backlog is matched and everyone is notified. No lost sign-ups, no dead link, and heavy compute never has to live in the cloud.

7 · Never lose a sign-up — durable registrations

The first, cheapest win — even before you stand up a relay — is to stop keeping registrations in RAM. Persist them to disk the instant they arrive, and resume on next launch.

What to store

  • A tiny SQLite DB, e.g. ~/.phorg/events/<event>.db
  • registrations: id, name, contact, selfie blob (or path), status (pending → matched → delivered), created_at
  • results: registration_id, matched photo paths, scores, album path
  • Reuse the existing per-folder embedding cache (hashcache.py) so re-scans stay fast

How it behaves

  • Selfie submitted → row written immediately (survives shutdown)
  • On app launch → load pending rows, resume matching automatically
  • Idempotent: re-running never double-delivers
  • Retention/TTL job purges the event after N days (privacy)
# minimal schema — durable registration queue
CREATE TABLE registrations(
  id         TEXT PRIMARY KEY,
  event      TEXT,
  name       TEXT,
  contact    TEXT,          -- email or phone for notification
  selfie     BLOB,          -- or a path under ~/.phorg/events/<event>/
  status     TEXT DEFAULT 'pending',   -- pending|matched|delivered|expired
  created_at INTEGER,
  matched_at INTEGER
);
CREATE TABLE results(
  reg_id  TEXT, path TEXT, score REAL,
  PRIMARY KEY(reg_id, path)
);
This alone upgrades the current behaviour from "close the app = forget everyone" to "close the app = the queue waits, and processes when you reopen." It's a small, local change with no cloud required — the foundation everything else builds on.

8 · Async delivery — how the guest gets told

If results aren't instant, the guest must be reached later. That contact field turns a dead link into a promise you keep.

✉️ Email

Cheapest, universal. Send "your photos from {event} are ready" with a secure, expiring link. Use a transactional provider (SES / Resend / Postmark).

💬 WhatsApp

Highest open rates in India. Use the WhatsApp Business API (or a click-to-chat + template) to deliver the album link — matches how the market already shares photos.

🔔 Web push / SMS

If the guest keeps the PWA open, web-push "your photos are ready." SMS as a fallback where WhatsApp isn't available.

Link security: every delivery link is a signed, per-guest token with an expiry (mirror the existing guest-token model). It reveals only that guest's album — never the folder or other people's photos.

9 · How to implement it — mapped to the current code

Phased, so each step ships value on its own. Nothing here throws away what exists; it extends server.py, vision.py and tunnel.py.

1

Persist registrations locally (no cloud yet)

Add the SQLite queue from §7. Write a row the moment a selfie hits /api/share/find/start; move _SHARE["guests"] and _JOBS state into it. On startup, reload pending and resume. Result: shutdown no longer loses anyone.

2

Capture contact + add a delivery step

Add name + email/phone to the guest form. When a match finishes, send the "ready" notification with a signed album link. Result: late/offline guests get their photos without the link being live 24×7.

3

Stand up the always-on relay (option ③)

A small always-on service (tiny VPS or serverless + object store) that (a) serves a stable branded URL instead of the throwaway *.trycloudflare.com, and (b) exposes POST /register that durably queues selfies. The PC polls GET /registrations?since=… and posts back results. Generalise tunnel.py into a "relay client." Result: the link never dies; sign-ups are accepted while the PC is off.

4

Add Tier B instant matching (opt-in)

While online, the PC publishes embeddings + thumbnails (from vision.py / the hash cache) to the relay. The relay runs the lightweight cosine match (same maths as best_score) and returns matches + previews instantly. Full-res downloads stream from the PC when up, or a short-TTL encrypted cache. Result: instant results anytime, without originals in the cloud.

5

Lifecycle & retention

Per-event expiry (extend the existing expires field), auto-purge on the relay and locally, and a host dashboard: who registered, matched, delivered, pending. Result: clean privacy story + operational visibility.

Relay API sketch

# Always-on relay — the only piece that must never sleep.
POST /events/<id>/register      # guest: {selfies[], contact} -> queued (works PC-offline)
GET  /events/<id>/registrations  # host: pull new sign-ups since cursor
POST /events/<id>/results        # host: push {reg_id, matches[]} -> triggers notify
GET  /a/<signed-token>           # guest: their album (signed, expiring, per-guest)

# Tier B only (opt-in instant matching):
POST /events/<id>/index          # host: {embeddings[], thumbnails[]} (no full photos)
POST /events/<id>/match          # guest: {selfie} -> instant matches from published index
Reuse, don't rebuild: the guest UI, QR, token model, rate limiter and threshold logic already exist in server.py; the face maths lives in vision.py; tunnel.py already proves opt-in public exposure. The relay is a thin, mostly-stateless forwarder with a queue — the genuinely new piece is small.

12 · Verdict & recommended path

Don't keep the laptop on. Keep a tiny relay on. Decouple registration from matching: an always-on relay owns the stable link and durably queues sign-ups; the PC does private matching whenever it's online and notifies guests. Add instant matching (Tier B) only for hosts who opt in.

Do this, in order:

  • ✅ Ship persistence first (§7) — SQLite queue so a shutdown never loses a sign-up. Local-only, no cloud, immediate win.
  • ✅ Add contact + async delivery (§8) — "we'll notify you," signed expiring album links.
  • ⭐ Stand up the always-on relay (option ③) — stable URL + store-and-forward queue. This is the real fix.
  • ⚡ Offer Tier B instant matching (option ④) — embeddings + thumbnails only, for hosts who want cloud-app speed.
  • 🧹 Enforce lifecycle — per-event TTL and auto-purge everywhere; a host dashboard for status.

Avoid the full cloud gallery (option ⑤) as a default — it throws away the privacy + zero-cost edge that makes this product different. Keep it as a clearly-labelled premium tier only.
